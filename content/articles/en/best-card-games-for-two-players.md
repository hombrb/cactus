# Cactus: The Best Two-Player Card Game You Have Never Heard Of

You want a card game for two that goes beyond Gin Rummy and War. Cactus is a memory-and-bluff game where you start without knowing your own cards, peek and swap your way to the lowest score, and bet your memory on a final "Cactus" call. It plays in 5 to 10 minutes per round, it needs nothing but a standard 52-card deck (or a phone), and it gets better the more you play against the same person.

The game goes by [many names](./cabo-cambio-golf-cactus-same-game.md): Dutch in France, Tamalou in Quebec, Cabo in Belgium and the backpacker circuit, Cambio in Latin America. It belongs to the [Golf family](https://www.pagat.com/draw/golf.html) of card games. The two-player version is where it shines brightest.


## Why Cactus works for two

Most card games designed for groups lose something at two players. Cactus gains something. Here's why.

**You can't hide.** In a four-player game, a power card like the 9 (peek at an opponent's card) could target anyone. At two players, every offensive power is aimed at you. When your opponent plays a Jack for a blind swap, they're reaching into your hand. When they peek with a 10, they're studying your cards. The pressure is constant and mutual.

**Snap is a pure duel.** The quick-discard mechanic (called "snap" or "defausse rapide") lets you slam a card from your layout onto the discard pile at any time, as long as it matches the top card's rank. At two players, there's no third party to beat you to it. It's a reaction-time contest between you and one other person, and the stakes are high: get it right and you shrink your hand, get it wrong and you take a penalty card.

**The call is personal.** When you announce "Cactus" (meaning you believe your score is the lowest), you're looking one person in the eye. You have one opponent to read, one set of moves to reconstruct. If you're wrong, your score doubles.

**Rounds are fast.** With two players, turns cycle quickly. A round takes 5 to 8 minutes. You can play a full match (to 100 cumulative points) in under 30 minutes, and each round teaches you something new about how the other person plays.


## Learn in 3 minutes

Deal 4 cards face down to each player in a 2x2 grid. The layout stays fixed. Don't move your cards around.

Put the rest of the deck in the center as the stock. Flip the top card face up to start the discard pile.

Each player peeks at their two nearest cards (the ones closest to them), once, then puts them back face down. These are the only two cards you know.

**Card values.** Red King (hearts, diamonds) = 0 (the best). Ace = 1. Number cards = face value. Jack = 11. Queen = 12. Black King (spades, clubs) = 13 (the worst, but carries the strongest power).

**Your turn.** Two options:
1. Draw from the stock. Look at the card. Either swap it into one of your grid slots (the replaced card goes to the discard, no power triggers) or discard it directly (if it has a power, the power triggers now).
2. Take the top discard card. You must swap it into your grid. No power triggers.

**Powers** (trigger only when you draw from stock and discard):
- 7 or 8: peek at one of your own cards
- 9 or 10: peek at one of your opponent's cards
- Jack or Queen: blind swap (exchange a card with your opponent without looking at either)
- Black King: look at one of yours and one of theirs, then decide whether to swap

**Quick discard (snap).** At any moment, slam a card from your grid onto the discard if its rank matches the top card. Right: the card is gone forever, that slot is empty. Wrong: the card goes back and you draw a penalty card face down.

**Calling Cactus.** After your turn, you can announce "Cactus." Your opponent gets one more turn. Then all cards are revealed. If you're strictly the lowest: you score 0. If you're tied or higher: your score doubles. Play continues over multiple rounds until someone hits 100 cumulative points. Lowest total wins.

For the complete rules and variant matrix, see the [full rules article](./cactus-card-game-rules.md).


## One phone, two players

The Cactus app was designed for exactly this. It's a free web app (a PWA) that runs in any browser. No app store, no account, no internet needed after the first visit.

The phone lies flat on the table between you, in portrait orientation. The screen splits: each player sees their half right-side up. Your cards appear at the edge closest to you. Long-press to peek at a card privately (the screen blocks the other player's view). Swipe toward the center to attempt a quick discard.

The UI is in French, but the game is visual enough that it barely matters. Quick translation: "Jouer sur ce telephone" means Play on this phone. "Piocher" means Draw. "Regles" means Rules.


## What makes it different from other two-player card games

**Gin Rummy** is about building sets and runs with full knowledge of your hand. In Cactus, you don't know your own cards. That changes every decision.

**Speed and Spit** are reflex games. Cactus has the snap mechanic for fast reactions, but the core game is about memory and deduction played at a deliberate pace.

**War** has zero decisions. You flip cards and watch. Cactus is all decisions: which card to keep, which power to use, when to snap, when to call.

**Uno** is about shedding cards as fast as possible. Cactus is about minimizing a hidden score you can only partially see.

The thing that makes Cactus unique in the two-player space is the hidden information. You're managing a hand you can't fully see, trying to improve it through peeks and swaps, and then you have to bet on your own memory being right. That tension doesn't exist in any of the classics.
